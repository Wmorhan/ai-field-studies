# Before everything starts...

## Roles are changing

We are the architects now.

Someone needs to hold the big picture, the plan, the clarity — and that someone is us. When that clarity is missing, the AI guesses. And that's where things start going wrong.

It's not a master/slave relationship. It's more like a permanent feedback loop — one where the quality of what we put in determines the quality of what comes out.

## Ways of working are changing

Depending on what you do day to day, the shift feels different.

For developers, the change might be less dramatic — maybe a shift in methodology, or stepping into more of an architect role.

For architects, it means getting closer to the code again. Decisions have more immediate, visible consequences.

For everyone else: welcome to something genuinely life-changing.

## Speed is changing

This is the part that catches people off guard. The AI moves fast. That means bad decisions get multiplied quickly.

What we've learned: constant validation is no longer optional. Frequent check-ins matter:

* Are we still aligned with project principles?
* Are the changes we're accepting actually right?
* Does the architecture still hold?

Speed is a feature. Unchecked speed is a risk.
